Activity 4.1.1 - Binary Numbers Magic Trick

Think of a number between 1 and 31. Answer a few questions, and the computer will read your mind.

How to perform the trick

  1. Ask someone to think of a number between 1 and 31, and keep it a secret inside their head!
  2. Show them each of the five cards below, one at a time. For each card, ask if their number is on it.
  3. If they say yes, put the card in the "yes" pile. If they say no, put it in the "no" pile.
  4. Add up the first number on each card in the "yes" pile. That's their number!

For example, if the "yes" pile holds the cards that start with 2, 8, and 4, their number is 2 + 8 + 4 = 14.

Don't tell anyone how you did it! In class, one student from each group learns the trick, then goes back and performs it for their group until someone figures out how it works.

The five cards

Spoiler warning! Try to figure out the trick on your own first.

It's binary!

Look at the first number on each card: 16, 8, 4, 2, 1. Those are the place values of a 5-bit binary number. Every number from 1 to 31 can be written as a sum of some of those values, and there's only one way to do it.

Each card holds exactly the numbers that have a 1 in that card's place. So when someone says "yes" to a card, they're telling you that bit is a 1. A "no" means it's a 0. Add up the place values of the 1s and you've converted their secret binary number back to decimal.
